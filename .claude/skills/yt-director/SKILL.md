---
name: yt-director
description: Turn the workspace shotlog and the motion/ and quality/ labels into an edit list (edit.json) with a rule-based, deterministic director — no LLM and no vision model. It ranks candidates by the shotlog's fused content score, refuses near-duplicate shots (never two similar segments, never back to back), gives every slot a target length from the brief's pacing with a min/max, keeps hero and complete semantic-move slots whole up to a cap, cuts inside the steady range, treats eligible still photos as first-class candidates (2-4 s photo slots with a deterministic Ken Burns move), lays the slots on a chronological or score-ordered timeline (photos mixed with clips by capture time) and writes edit.json validated against schemas/edit.schema.json. Use when the user asks to build an edit, a rough cut or a recap from analysed footage, wants an edit.json, or asks which moments the director would pick. Trigger phrases - "fă edit.json", "construiește montajul din footage", "directorul alege cadrele", "build an edit from the analysis", "pick the cut windows", "generează lista de montaj". Not for analysing footage (that is `ingest`/`motion`/`quality`), describing content (that is `describe`), indexing the shotlog (that is `index`) or rendering the MP4 (that is `render`).
compatibility: Run from the repository root. Needs the repo .venv; CPU only, no GPU and no network. Reads ingest.json, shotlog.json, motion/<stem>.json and quality/<stem>.json from the workspace read-only and writes edit.json (or --out) atomically. Deterministic: the same inputs and flags always produce the same edit.

# yt-director

Pick the cut windows of an edit from the shotlog and the labels the analysis tools already wrote. Rule-based and deterministic, no model is called. It reads the workspace only and writes `edit.json`, the single source of truth every output adapter reads. All commands run from the repository root.

## Step 1: direct an edit

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/director.py --workspace /path/to/workspace \
  --brief "about 90 seconds, calm start, more energy towards the end" \
  --target-duration-s 90 --order chronological --pacing calm-to-energy --json
```

- `--workspace` is required and must hold `ingest.json`, `motion/<stem>.json` and `quality/<stem>.json`; `shotlog.json` is optional but, when present, supplies the content score, the description and the reason. A missing `ingest.json` is fatal; a clip without a `quality` document is skipped with a warning; a clip without a `motion` document still contributes hold windows and uses the quality segment bounds for moves.
- `--out` (default `WORKSPACE/edit.json`) is the edit list to write. `--brief` is stored verbatim in `edit.json`; it is not parsed into flags.
- `--target-duration-s` (default 90) sets the edit length. `--order` is `chronological` (default) or `score`. `--pacing` is `calm-to-energy` (default) or `uniform`.
- `--min-slot-s`, `--max-slot-s`, `--important-max-s`, `--calm-slot-s` and `--energy-slot-s` are overrides — by default (no value) each comes from the pacing table derived from `--target-duration-s`: `--min-slot-s` drops shorter windows, `--max-slot-s` caps a slot, `--important-max-s` is the longest a hero or semantic-move slot may be (6.0 s ceiling), `--calm-slot-s` and `--energy-slot-s` are the opening and closing slot targets in `calm-to-energy`. `--xfade-s` (default 0.3) is the crossfade written into every non-first slot.
- `--beats FILE` (optional) is the beat grid JSON written by `scripts/beats.py --out` (keys `beat_s`, `downbeat_s`, `beat_period_s`/`duration_s`, either under `measurement` or at the top level; the times are absolute seconds in the music, which starts at timeline 0). With it the director snaps every timeline cut to the nearest beat (max shift 0.4 of a beat period, else a `cut k: not snapped to a beat` warning and the cut stays) and writes `transition: {"type": "cut", "duration_s": 0}` on every non-first slot, because a music edit cuts on the beat. Without `--beats` nothing changes.
- `--speed-ramp` (default off) lets the middle half of long camera moves play up to 1.2x; without it every slot has `middle_speed` 1.0.
- `--allow-similar` lets visually similar shots into the same edit; they are then only forbidden from being adjacent. Without it, a shot similar to any already chosen shot is skipped.
- `--force` rewrites even when the output is up to date. `--json` prints the machine-readable summary.
- On any error (missing workspace, no usable candidates, schema violation, write failure) it prints one `[error] ...` line to stderr and exits 1. It never asks questions.

## Step 2: read the result

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/director.py --workspace /path/to/workspace --json
```

The `--json` output is a single object, e.g. `{"status": "written", "out": "/abs/workspace/edit.json", "slots": 12, "total_duration_s": 87.4, "target_duration_s": 90.0, "warnings": [], "wall_time_s": 0.51, "llm_cost_usd": 0.0, "beat_snap": null}`. `status` is `"written"` or `"skipped"`; `wall_time_s` is the seconds the run took and `llm_cost_usd` is always `0.0` (this tool never calls a model); `beat_snap` is the `--beats` snap tally (`{"beats", "snapped", "unsnapped"}`) or null without a grid. Without `--json` it prints one line per slot (`v1  clip.mp4  12.50-21.00  0.72  interest 60 · travel_backward by verdict · move · clean · calm opening`; a photo prints `p1  photo_01.jpg  photo  3.00s  0.77  lighthouse · calm opening`), then `total X.Xs of target Y.Ys`, then the warnings.

## How the director decides

- **Candidates.** Every quality segment with role `hold` becomes its own window; `move`, `transition_in` and `transition_out` use the overlapping motion move's handle window (falling back to the steady range, then the move bounds, then the segment bounds). When `shotlog.json` carries **content beats** for a segment (`beat_of` set), the segment contributes one candidate per beat instead: each beat's window is the beat's own range intersected with the move window, so a 134 s tunnel transit can offer its approach and its exit as separate cuttable windows. A quality segment whose role is `accidental`, `setup` or `teardown` is never a candidate, whatever the shotlog says — the director decides here. Windows shorter than `--min-slot-s` are dropped, and overlapping windows inside one clip keep the higher-scored one. **Photo shots** in the shotlog (`media_type: "photo"`, key `<counter>/p1`) are candidates too: they pass the same rules (eligible, never `accidental`/`setup`/`teardown`; a photo is always `hold`), join the same slot budget and the same scoring/order paths, and each contributes at most one slot. A burst-suppressed photo (`eligible: false`) is never a candidate. Photos have no same-take windows, no phrases and no overlap guard — each photo is its own file.
- **Photo slots.** A photo slot is a still held **2-4 s** (`duration_s` is the pacing target for that slot clamped to `[2.0, 4.0]`, default 3.0) with a deterministic Ken Burns move from the photo's `subject_position`: missing position → `zoom_in` focus `0.5, 0.5`; centre box (`|x-0.5| <= 0.15` and `|y-0.5| <= 0.2`) → `zoom_in` focus at the subject; otherwise `pan_left` when `x < 0.5` else `pan_right`, focus at the subject; `zoom_out` when the photo's `tags` contain `close-up` or `closeup` (the subject fills the frame). `amount` is `0.08`. The slot's `reason` is the photo's hook or summary plus the pacing word. `sources[]` gains one row per used photo (`media_type: "photo"`, `duration_s: null`, path resolved from ingest `photos[]`).
- **Content score.** When `shotlog.json` is present the director joins it to the candidates by shot key (`<clip counter>/<segment id>`, e.g. `0081/s7`, or `<clip counter>/<segment id><beat id>`, e.g. `0072/s7b1`): a shot with `eligible: false` is not a candidate, the candidate's score is the shotlog's fused score, the candidate's `role` and `motion_label` are the shotlog row's effective labels (the verdict already won there), the slot's `description` is the shot's description (null when it has none) and the slot's `reason` is the shot's reason plus the pacing word. The cut window still follows the quality segment's role. `similar_to`, `hook` and `hero` are kept for the diversity rule and the human output but are not written to `edit.json`. Without a shotlog (or for a segment with no shot row) the director falls back to scoring the labels and building the reason from them, and `description` stays null.
- **Label score (fallback).** Base by role (hold 0.55, move 0.50, transition 0.35) plus `0.25 * confidence`, a steadiness bonus (`no_shaking +0.15`, `minimal_shaking +0.08`, `static +0.10`, `unsteady -0.10`, `very_unsteady -0.20`), a defect penalty (`-min(0.4, sum(value * weight))`, horizon weighted 0.5) and `+0.10` when the segment is clean. The result is clamped to 0..1.
- **Diversity.** `similar_to` is treated as symmetric. By default a candidate whose shot is similar to any already chosen shot is skipped (with a warning). With `--allow-similar` similar shots may both enter but never back to back: a candidate is skipped when its shot is similar to the slot immediately before or after it at the insert position. After assembly, any remaining adjacent similar pair is reported as a warning.
- **Pacing.** The slot lengths come from a pacing table that is a pure function of the target duration `T`: linearly interpolated between the anchors `T=20 → slot 1.5/2.0/2.5 s, hero cap 3.5 s, 10 slots`, `T=90 → slot 2.0/3.6/5.0 s, hero cap 6.0 s, 25 slots` and `T=180 → slot 2.5/5.0/6.0 s, hero cap 6.0 s, 32 slots`, clamped to the 20 s row below 20 s and to the 180 s row above 180 s. `hero_max = min(6.0, slot_target * 1.75)`; `n_slots = clamp(round(T / slot_target), 4, 32)`. With `calm-to-energy` the target of slot `i` of `n` is a linear interpolation from `slot_max` (first, or `--calm-slot-s` when given) down to `slot_min` (last, or `--energy-slot-s` when given); with `uniform` every target is `slot_target`. Every target is clamped to `[slot_min, slot_max]` (or the explicit `--min-slot-s`/`--max-slot-s`). The targets are not scaled to sum to the target duration.
- **Timeline.** The clips are laid end-to-end in chronological order and the total content time is split into `n_slots` equal buckets; each bucket takes its highest-scored unused candidate whose content window overlaps it, trimmed to the slot's target duration (shorter windows stay whole, never padded). A photo's position on that content timeline is its `capture_time` mapped into the same span the clips use (a photo with no capture time sorts at the end), so chronological order mixes photos and clips by capture time. Wall-clock gaps between clips do not create empty buckets. An empty bucket is skipped with a warning. `--order score` instead fills the slots highest-score first (photos included, score first).
- **Fill pass.** When the picked slots sum to less than the target duration and unused candidates remain, the director keeps adding the highest-scored unused candidate (tie: earliest content position), trimmed to the last interpolated target and checked by the overlap guard, until the sum reaches `--target-duration-s` or runs out. Chronological order inserts each fill slot at its content position; `--order score` appends it. A candidate dropped by the overlap guard counts as used.
- **Cut window.** Every slot is cut inside the steady range: `in_s` from the move's `handle_in_s`/`steady_start_s`, `out_s` where the steady motion ends (`steady_end_s`, falling back to `handle_out_s`/`end_s` only when it is missing) — the window never extends onto aimless movement after the move — never past the window and never shorter than `--min-slot-s`.
- **Important slots.** A candidate is important when the shotlog row says `hero: true` (any role), or its effective `semantic_motion` is `orbit`/`reveal`/`tracking`/`pov`/`travel`, or its effective `motion_label` is `orbit`/`orbit_candidate`/`reveal`/`tracking`/`pov` — the last two only when the effective `motion_label` is not `static` or `minor_motion` (a static wake frame tagged `travel` is not a camera move). Effective labels are the shotlog row's when there is one, else the candidate's own. An important slot ignores its pacing target and keeps its whole cut window, capped at `--important-max-s`: `out_s` is the window end and `in_s = max(window_start, out_s - important_max_s)`, so the tail (the move's completion and its landing) survives and the head cut stays inside the steady phase. A window shorter than the cap is kept whole, never padded. `--max-slot-s` does not cut an important slot below `--important-max-s`; the two caps are independent and `--important-max-s` wins for important slots. Ordinary slots are trimmed by the pacing target exactly as before.
- **Move length policy (`common/moves.py`, D2-11, ramp optional and peak-centred cuts since D2-12).** After the slots are chosen and merged and before the beat snap, `apply_move_policy` fits every slot to the seconds rule. A *move* is a slot whose role is `move`/`transition_in`/`transition_out` or that is important, unless its motion label is `static`/`minor_motion`. A move keeps as much of its candidate window as its on-screen budget allows (never outside the window, never overlapping another slot of the same take). An ordinary move's budget is its pacing length (capped at `slot_max`); at most 15 % (rounded) of the slots (minimum one), the best scores among the moves that want more than `slot_max`, may run up to `hero_max` on screen and are reported as `exception: ...` warnings. Every other slot, every static or hold slot (a static hero included) and every photo is clamped to `slot_max`. **Normal speed is the default**: `middle_speed` stays 1.0 unless `--speed-ramp` is given, which lets the middle half of a move play up to 1.2x (head and tail quarters real time; on-screen length `0.25L + 0.5L/s + 0.25L`). The beat snap then absorbs its shifts in on-screen time and never lengthens a slot past its cap, so cuts still land on beats.
- **Peak-centred cuts (`common/peak.py`, D2-12).** When a window is longer than the allowed on-screen length, the cut is centred on the shot's best moment, clamped inside the candidate window. The peak comes from, in order: `peak_s` in `shotlog.json` (vision, from describe), the maximum smoothed `scene_activity` in `motion/` inside the window (`motion`), the window middle (`middle`). A landing phrase keeps its landing rule (the cut ends at or after the landing floor), which wins over the peak and adds a warning. Each slot records `peak_s`, `peak_source` and `centred`; `meta.speed_ramp` records the ramp setting and a changed value makes the director rewrite the edit.
- **Move + landing-hold phrases.** Two consecutive quality segments `M` then `H` of one clip where `M` is a `move`/`transition_in`/`transition_out`, `H` is a `hold` and `H.start_s - M.end_s <= 0.75` form a landing pair. The move's usable form is then the phrase `[M handle-in, H.end_s]`: the move alone is dropped, and the phrase and the hold alone both stay in the pool (they overlap, so the higher-scored one survives). The phrase's score is the move's score — the landing is grammar, not extra content — so the phrase competes on the move's worth and the hold alone wins exactly when it is the better shot; on an exact tie the phrase wins. The phrase's description/reason/shot key come from the move; its role, motion label and steadiness stay the move's. A phrase is trimmed for pacing like any slot: the end cut always lands inside the hold (`out_s >= H.start_s + 0.75`, never past `H.end_s`) and the head is trimmed so the slot respects its target, never before `M`'s handle-in; when the head trim fires a `phrase trimmed from the head` warning is added. A phrase slot longer than `--max-slot-s` (a safety net that should not fire) adds a `phrase kept through landing` warning. A move with no landing hold keeps its own handle window.
- **Same-take continuity.** A take is one source clip. Adjacent slots from the same clip must be source-contiguous (their windows touch within 0.5 s); a finished slot that would sit next to a non-contiguous same-clip slot is skipped with a `same-take jump cut avoided` warning in every pick path — the check runs on the slot's final cut window, after trimming, so a trim can never create a jump cut. When the blocked slot scores strictly higher than the conflicting same-take slot, it replaces it instead: the weaker slot is removed, the insert position recomputed and re-checked, and a `same-take upgrade: <new> replaces <old>` warning is added — the take's slot is its best window. Adjacent source-contiguous same-clip slots are merged into one slot (covering both, keeping the higher score and its reason) so no crossfade splits a continuous piece of a take. Non-adjacent same-take slots are allowed — a different take sits between them.
- **Reason.** With a shotlog it is the shot's reason plus the pacing word (`calm opening` when the wall-clock position is `<= 0.4`, `energy build` when it is `>= 0.6` and the role is not `hold`, `late hold` when it is `>= 0.6` and the role is `hold`, `neutral` otherwise), de-duplicated. Without a shotlog it is built from the labels joined with ` · `: role, motion label, steadiness, `clean` or the defect names, and the pacing word. Example: `hold · static · clean · calm opening`.
- **Beat snap (`--beats`).** After the slots are chosen and merged, the timeline is the plain sum of the slot lengths (a beat grid makes every join a hard cut). For each cut — the end of each slot, final cut included — the director finds the nearest beat and, when it is within `0.4 * beat_period_s`, moves the cut onto it by extending or trimming the previous slot: a video slot's `out_s` moves within `[in_s + 0.5, window_out_s]` (and never below the phrase floor `landing_start_s + 0.75`; an important/hero slot is left whole), a photo's `duration_s` moves within `[2.0, 4.0]`, preferring a beat count that fits (e.g. 4–8 beats at 120 BPM). When no beat is in reach or the slot cannot absorb the shift, a `cut k: not snapped to a beat (nearest X s away)` warning is added and the cut stays. `--json` reports `beat_snap: {"beats": int, "snapped": int, "unsnapped": int}` (null without `--beats`).

## Notes

- The edit list is `edit.json`; `render.py` and the optional Kdenlive export are adapters that read it. This tool never renders and never touches the source clips or photos. Video slots keep ids `v1..` and photo slots use `p1..`, each in timeline order of its kind; both validate against `schemas/edit.schema.json` (`video[]` is `oneOf` video_slot | photo_slot).
- Re-run safety: when `--out` exists, validates against the edit schema and is newer than every input file (including `shotlog.json`), the tool writes nothing and reports `status: "skipped"`.
- The director is deliberately crude: it only sees the shotlog and the labels, so its faults (short windows, empty buckets when a stretch of footage has no usable segment) are the priorities for the later director phases.
